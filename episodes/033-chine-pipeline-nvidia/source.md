# Source — China's $3 billion-plus pipeline of Nvidia-powered AI data centres

- URL : https://www.reuters.com/technology/chinas-3-billion-plus-pipeline-nvidia-powered-ai-data-centres-2026-11-13/
- Publié par : Reuters

- Date de publication : 2026-11-13

## Fiche

### Faits
- Une enquête menée par Reuters révèle l'existence de plus de 3 milliards de dollars (plus de 23 milliards de yuans) de projets d'infrastructures de calcul et de centres de données d'IA en Chine conçus autour des processeurs Nvidia sous restrictions américaines.
- L'investigation s'appuie sur l'analyse approfondie de plus de 150 avis de marchés publics, plans d'investissement, contrats de construction et déclarations financières d'entreprises chinoises.
- Les projets recensés s'étendent des pôles technologiques côtiers prospères jusqu'aux provinces reculées de l'ouest chinois.
- La demande ne se limite pas aux géants privés de la tech mais irrigue des entreprises soutenues par des gouvernements locaux, des universités et des centres de calcul régionaux.
- L'enveloppe chiffrée englobe des investissements planifiés, des contrats signés et des installations achevées, couvrant les bâtiments, les réseaux d'alimentation électrique et l'infrastructure globale, et ne reflète pas uniquement l'achat isolé de puces.
- Ces révélations surviennent au lendemain de la démission de Jensen Huang de son poste de PDG de Nvidia, déclenchée par un scandale relatif à sa liaison avec une femme d'affaires soupçonnée de liens avec les services de renseignement chinois.
- Jensen Huang plaidait constamment pour l'assouplissement des contrôles à l'exportation et avait affirmé l'an dernier que la part de marché de Nvidia en Chine était passée de 95 % à zéro.
- Nvidia a exclu les revenus des centres de données chinois de ses prévisions financières en raison de l'incertitude réglementaire, et a affirmé en août 2026 être de fait exclue de ce marché.
- Pourtant, les documents consultés démontrent que des clients chinois continuent de planifier l'acquisition de processeurs Nvidia et, dans plusieurs cas, exploitent déjà du matériel sous embargo.
- Un rapport du cabinet IDC cité par Reuters estimait que Nvidia représentait encore 34 % des livraisons d'accélérateurs d'IA en Chine au premier semestre 2026.
- Les projets identifiés reposent sur des processeurs soumis à restrictions : H100, H200 et H800.
- Dans le comté de Xianyou (Fujian), un projet de centre de calcul IA estimé à 6,2 milliards de yuans (873 millions de dollars) cherche des partenaires commerciaux et prévoit des puces Nvidia H100 pour le développement de modèles d'IA à grande échelle.
- Dans la province du Shaanxi, une filiale du groupe d'ingénierie public Sinomach a remporté un contrat au sein d'un projet de centre de calcul de 3 milliards de yuans spécifiant des processeurs H100 et H200.
- Dans le Jilin, un prospectus officiel d'avril 2026 décrit un projet de 2,26 milliards de yuans associant des H100 à des processeurs conçus en Chine, financé à 80 % par des prêts bancaires.
- En Mongolie intérieure, un programme de 1,5 milliard de yuans projette l'installation de 625 serveurs Nvidia H200, la construction de deux premiers bâtiments ayant démarré en juin 2026.
- Dans le Gansu, une entreprise locale a attribué en juillet 2026 un contrat de construction de 1,16 milliard de yuans à un consortium mené par Shaanxi Construction Engineering pour un centre prévoyant environ 3 072 processeurs H100 et H200.
- À Wuxi (Jiangsu), les autorités municipales ont déclaré en mars 2025 la mise en service opérationnelle d'un cluster de calcul équipé de processeurs Nvidia H800.
- Dans le Fujian, l'entreprise cotée Damingcheng a dévoilé en juin 2026 qu'une coentreprise avait assemblé un cluster H800, après un accord initial de services de calcul de 759 millions de yuans écourté.
- Des achats ciblés concernent également la recherche académique : l'Université de Shenzhen a commandé trois H100 en septembre 2026, tandis que l'Université polytechnique du Nord-Ouest (Northwestern Polytechnical University), inscrite sur liste noire américaine, a acquis deux H200 en octobre 2026.
- Ni le ministère chinois du Commerce ni Nvidia n'ont répondu immédiatement aux sollicitations de Reuters.
- Les projets identifiés par Reuters ne présentent aucun lien avéré avec l'entreprise Megaspeed ni avec les allégations visant Jensen Huang.

### Disponibilité
- Qui : Collectivités locales chinoises, centres régionaux de calcul, universités et entreprises d'État chinoises.
- Où : Déployé à travers la Chine, des provinces côtières (Fujian, Jiangsu) aux régions occidentales et septentrionales (Shaanxi, Gansu, Mongolie intérieure, Jilin).
- Quand : Projets à des stades variés — certains clusters H800 déjà en service (Wuxi depuis mars 2025, Fujian), chantiers de construction entamés (Mongolie intérieure, Gansu), autres dossiers au stade de prospection ou d'appels d'offres (Xianyou, Jilin).
- Prix : Budget global cumulé dépassant 23 milliards de yuans (~3,2 milliards de dollars) ; les documents publics ne divulguent pas le coût unitaire des puces acquises ni d'éventuels surcoûts d'intermédiation.

### Zones floues
- Canaux d'approvisionnement et livraison réelle : L'enquête ne permet pas de déterminer par quels circuits le matériel restreint parvient jusqu'aux entités chinoises, ni si les processeurs stipulés dans les appels d'offres (tels que les 3 072 puces du Gansu) ont été effectivement livrés.
- Part exacte du matériel Nvidia dans les budgets : Le montant global de 3,2 milliards de dollars inclut le génie civil, l'alimentation électrique et les bâtiments ; il ne mesure pas uniquement le volume d'achats de puces Nvidia.
- Impact du départ de Jensen Huang et durcissement des sanctions : La démission du PDG prive Nvidia d'un relais d'influence clé contre les restrictions, augmentant le risque de retards majeurs, de ruptures de fourniture ou d'obligation de reconception des projets autour de puces locales.
- Portée des licences d'exportation individuelles : Malgré la procédure d'examen au cas par cas instaurée en janvier 2026 par l'administration américaine pour le H200, l'enquête ne précise pas si des autorisations ont été légalement accordées ou si ces infrastructures contournent les voies officielles.

### Huang exit and US scrutiny imperil $3 billion in Chinese AI projects reliant on Nvidia / China's $3 billion-plus pipeline of Nvidia-powered AI data centres
By Eduardo Baptista

BEIJING, Nov 13 (Reuters) - Nvidia has said US export controls have effectively shut it out of China's AI data-centre market, but a Reuters review has identified more than $3 billion in Chinese computing projects built around the American chipmaker's restricted processors.

The projects, stretching from China's wealthy coastal technology hubs to remote western provinces, reveal the scale of continuing demand for Nvidia hardware even as Washington seeks to curb Beijing's access to the technology powering the global artificial-intelligence boom.

The Reuters review examined more than 150 Chinese procurement notices, investment plans, construction contracts and corporate disclosures, identifying projects and equipment agreements with a combined reported value exceeding 23 billion yuan ($3.2 billion).

The findings paint a detailed picture of how deeply Nvidia technology remains embedded in China's AI expansion. They reveal the company's technology reaches well beyond China's largest tech firms, into local government-backed ventures, universities and regional computing centres.

The estimate includes planned investments and signed contracts as well as completed installations. It covers buildings, power systems and other infrastructure, and does not reflect spending on Nvidia chips alone.

China's commerce ministry and Nvidia did not immediately respond to Reuters request for comment.

The findings come a day after Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive amid a scandal over his relationship with a Chinese businesswoman suspected by US authorities of intelligence ties to Beijing.

Huang, a vocal advocate of easing export restrictions, said last year Nvidia's market share in China had fallen from 95% to zero. The company has also excluded China data-centre computing revenue from its forecasts, citing regulatory uncertainty.

That does not mean Chinese demand has disappeared. While Nvidia's statements address its own ability to compete and sell directly in China, the documents Reuters reviewed show Chinese customers continuing to plan purchases and, in some cases, operating restricted processors.

Sunny Cheung, an analyst at the Washington-based Jamestown Foundation, said Reuters' findings reinforce his own view on enforcement: "Washington must stop focusing solely on who buys the chips on the surface and start examining who ultimately uses the computing power—and for what purpose."

BILLIONS IN THE PIPELINE

Across China, developers and institutions are advancing billions of dollars in AI data-centre projects designed around restricted Nvidia H100, H200, and H800 processors. These range from massive regional computing hubs to retrofitted commercial sites - such as a facility in northwestern Gansu province that was expected to install thousands of Nvidia chips before year-end, though it remains unclear if those processors ever arrived.

While some facilities are already operational or under construction, operators are exposed to potential delays, supply disruptions, or costly redesigns around domestic chips as export controls tighten.

Huang's departure could deepen these vulnerabilities by weakening Nvidia's lobbying leverage as US lawmakers press for tighter controls.

WASHINGTON'S DILEMMA

Washington began restricting Nvidia's advanced AI processors in 2022 and subsequently tightened controls on modified chips developed for China, including the H800.

In January, the Trump administration moved to review H200 export licences for eligible Chinese customers individually, subject to conditions.

Nvidia's August filing said it was effectively excluded from China's data-centre computing market. But an IDC report cited by Reuters in October estimated Nvidia still accounted for 34% of Chinese AI-accelerator shipments in the first half of 2026.

The figures measure different things and do not establish how the restricted hardware identified in the Reuters investigation reached Chinese users.

The scrutiny has intensified since Huang's resignation on Thursday, following allegations involving Alice Huang, a former executive associated with Singapore-based Nvidia cloud partner Megaspeed.

Megaspeed has denied illegally diverting Nvidia chips to China. Nvidia's board launched an independent investigation, saying it had no reason to believe company secrets or US national security were compromised. There has been no public finding that Jensen Huang violated export-control laws.

The Chinese projects identified by Reuters have not been linked to Megaspeed or the allegations involving Huang.

### Factbox: Key projects in China's $3 billion-plus pipeline of Nvidia-powered AI data centres
By Eduardo Baptista

BEIJING, Nov 13 (Reuters) - A Reuters review has identified more than $3 billion in Chinese computing projects built around Nvidia's restricted processors, even as the American chipmaker has said US export controls have effectively shut it out of the AI data-centre market in the world's second-biggest economy.

The examination shows projects stretching from China's wealthy coastal technology hubs to remote western provinces, and underscores how deeply Nvidia's technology remains embedded in China's AI expansion.

The findings come a day after Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang, a vocal advocate of easing export restrictions, resigned as chief executive over a scandal.

Huang's departure could deepen vulnerabilities for Chinese companies exposed to potential delays and supply disruptions as US curbs tighten.

Below are details of projects the Reuters review identified:

BILLIONS AT STAKE

A proposed 6.2 billion yuan ($873 million) AI computing centre in coastal Fujian province, in Xianyou county, seeks commercial partners and specifies Nvidia H100 chips for a facility intended to support large-scale AI development.

In northwestern Shaanxi province, state-owned engineering group Sinomach said a subsidiary won a contract as part of a project for a 3 billion yuan computing centre specifying Nvidia H100 and H200 processors.

In northeastern Jilin province, an April government prospectus describes another 2.26 billion yuan project combining Nvidia H100 processors with Chinese-developed chips. The developer expects bank loans to finance about 80% of the investment.

In Inner Mongolia, a 1.5 billion-yuan development proposes installing 625 Nvidia H200 servers. Chinese industry reporting in June said construction had begun on two buildings in its first phase.

These plans highlight developers' reliance on US processors vulnerable to trade restrictions. Tighter curbs risk construction delays, inflated costs, or forced redesigns around domestic alternatives.

FROM SHOPPING CENTRE TO AI HUB

Other projects have advanced further.

In northwestern Gansu province, a local company awarded a 1.16 billion yuan construction contract in July for a computing centre that plans to install approximately 3,072 Nvidia processors, according to tender documents specifying H100 and H200 hardware.

The contract went to a consortium led by a subsidiary of state-owned Shaanxi Construction Engineering. The documents do not establish whether the processors have been delivered.

Other Chinese facilities are already using restricted Nvidia hardware.

In eastern Jiangsu province, the Wuxi municipal government said in March 2025 that a computing cluster using Nvidia H800 processors had entered operation.

In Fujian, Shanghai-listed Damingcheng disclosed in June that a computing venture had built an H800 cluster. It had signed a computing-services agreement originally worth 759 million yuan, although the contract ended early.

Smaller purchases show demand extending to research institutions. Shenzhen University awarded a contract for three H100 processors in September, while US-blacklisted Northwestern Polytechnical University bought two H200 chips the following month, Reuters reported separately.

(Reporting by Eduardo Baptista; Editing by Shri Navaratnam)
